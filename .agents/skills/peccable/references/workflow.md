# Workflow

Use this reference when the target, next intervention, design direction, or stopping point requires judgment.
The assignment defines the work; autonomy selects the method, not an indefinite mandate to improve the project.

## Continue from project context

Start with the latest request and relevant prior decisions. Inspect the named route, component, flow, or artifact and its immediate dependencies.
Consult project instructions, nearby implementations, tokens, content, assets, and available rendered evidence.
Use existing conventions; do not require a special brief, generated config, or context file.

Infer what is already settled:
- An existing form and backend contract establish fields and submission behavior unless the task changes them.
- A partial screen and agreed direction establish momentum; complete them rather than restarting discovery.
- Coherent neighboring screens establish identity without written design guidance.
- User-provided screenshots and reported failures establish real concerns; investigate causes rather than requiring reproduction before proceeding.

Separate evidence from assumptions: unfinished implementation may reveal intended behavior without proving it works.
Resolve doc/code conflicts using the request, current behavior, and relevant project authority; do not silently invent product truth.

Form a compact working brief internally for ordinary work or as the planning deliverable:
- **Job:** who arrives, their context, and what they need to do or understand.
- **Outcome:** requested change and observable success.
- **Boundary:** target, adjacent dependencies, identity commitments, and what stays untouched.
- **Material:** real content/assets/data, realistic ranges, and consequential states.
- **Approach:** structural or interaction decision and supporting evidence.
- **Evidence:** what is observable and what requires unavailable access.

A settled local task may need only three bullets; a multi-screen plan needs enough detail for another builder to execute without inventing critical decisions.
The brief is not obligatory documentation.

## Ask only consequential questions

Exhaust relevant reachable conversation, code, docs, and context before escalating missing intent, factual content, or authority.
Resolve reversible craft choices yourself; assumptions about product structure, capabilities, data shape, or required behavior are not merely design choices.
Ask for only the smallest missing material answer affecting the outcome or crossing an authority boundary; explain the blocked decision.

- Useful questions concern unresolved primary purpose, required functionality, contradictory factual content, genuine identity replacement, or permission for irreversible actions.
- Do not ask users to select design commands, CSS values, routine craft tactics, or preset aesthetic directions.
- Do not seek confirmation of precise requests or permission for already-authorized independent work.
- If a human is unavailable, state the exact missing fact, intent, or authority and blocked acceptance; no new artifact is required.
- Without waiting for approval, complete genuinely independent useful work within scope that assumes none of the missing answers.
- Keep dependent implementation blocked rather than supplying speculative structure or behavior. Separate finished work from blocked acceptance in the handoff.

## Choose the highest-leverage scoped intervention

Judge candidates by effect on the requested user outcome, evidence strength, and required breadth of change.
Use judgment, not a weighted scorecard.

Prefer causal fixes:
- If the primary action disappears on narrow screens, fix layout and access before polishing icons.
- If users cannot tell what to do next, improve hierarchy and action labels before adding animation.
- If an error destroys entered work, fix the state transition and recovery rather than softening the error copy alone.
- If every screen repeats the same token mistake, a scoped shared correction may be more coherent than local overrides; inspect its affected consumers.
- If a whole page is generic, reconsider its information sequence and focal evidence instead of decorating the same interchangeable template.

The smallest coherent change is not always the fewest lines: a complete changed flow may need loading, error, recovery, and responsive states.
Do not add unrelated cleanup, new features, global restyling, or performance architecture to a local design task.

## Decide how much invention is authorized

- **Refine:** preserve established identity, function, factual copy, and content; improve usability and readability.
- **Extend:** fill a missing role or surface using the incumbent system's visual and behavioral logic.
- **Replace:** when redesign or greenfield work is authorized, choose a new coherent direction while preserving product truth and stated commitments.

Extensions require an identifiable gap and existing system evidence. A new color, typeface, or material that rewrites recognizable product character replaces identity; it is not “polish.”
Local additions do not reopen global art direction; missing design documents do not erase visual authority.
Honor user-specified eras, palettes, fonts, materials, familiar conventions, and restrained treatments with full craft, not your preferred style.

Before a visual redesign alters routes, anchors, search/social metadata, analytics hooks, logos, or legal/consent content, inspect their existing contracts and dependencies.
Preserve them when outside the requested change; when a change is authorized, carry it through affected links, metadata, and consumers rather than silently breaking them.

For open composition, choose a clear thesis from the audience, subject, and visitor's job:
- What leads in the first viewport or screen?
- What does the visitor understand next, and where can they act?
- What real proof, artifact, or working mechanism makes this specific to the product?
- Which one dominant visual or interaction move supports that path?
- Can the direction carry real content, small screens, recovery states, and available assets?

Draw inspiration from the audience's graphic and screen traditions, publications, identity systems, notation, places, and rituals as well as physical tools and materials.
Compare genuinely distinct inspirations internally only when open composition needs alternatives, judging audience relevance and task clarity. A sketch or probe may help resolve that uncertainty; neither is required.
Borrow system discipline—hierarchy, rhythm, grouping, or navigation—rather than another direction's costume or copied motifs. Carry one coherent system through the surface.
Choose a viable approach yourself; do not stage a concept tournament or reopen settled direction for variety.
Execute the settled direction at its authorized scope and fidelity; do not replace it with a generic design because no user is present.
Change direction only when requirements or evidence justify it. Keep unresolved intent or authority blocked.

Match expression to the job of each surface:
- **Working tools:** prioritize familiar navigation, controls, task areas, and states. Tie filters to their data and keep charts and tables readable. Use type, palette, density, and notation for identity without obscuring operation.
- **Reading surfaces:** express identity through page framing and wayfinding; keep the reading column legible and uncluttered. Use the actual opening and demanding content, including code, tables, or diagrams, rather than ideal short samples.
- **Expressive surfaces:** integrate usable navigation, text, and primary actions into the composition. Implement required search, booking, or purchase behavior; painted controls or decorative links cannot replace it.

Borrow an instrument's or fictional interface's notation when it clarifies real information; do not imply capabilities with fake gauges, prompts, or switches.
Custom controls and strong styling remain valid when the brief supports them and their operation is clear and accessible. Typography, color, imagery, density, and motion serve the surface's job rather than competing as independent effects.

## Implement a complete scoped result

- Use existing components and framework patterns where they fit. Apply the improvement across relevant instances and states; do not leave half the surface in a second system.
- Confirm installed dependencies and versions before using their APIs or changing framework configuration; do not replace the project's stack merely for visual convenience.
- Implement required interactive techniques with the supported stack, relevant input methods, states, and accessible alternatives. Static visual imitations do not satisfy required behavior.
- Distinctiveness alone does not require effects, canvas, or new dependencies.
- If a required capability is unavailable, report the exact prerequisite rather than substitute a static image.
- Retain semantic structure, real data contracts, navigation, platform affordances, and accessible operation.
- Use actual content; stress the design with realistic short, typical, and long values.
- Do not invent product facts, capabilities, data contracts, or integrations to make the design appear finished.
- Clearly label genuinely illustrative material; it cannot supply missing facts or replace required working behavior.
- Respect requested fidelity and write boundaries. Requested prototypes can honestly demonstrate concepts; scaffolds, placeholders, and mocks are not completed implementation or production integrations.
- Planning-only permits no implementation or unsolicited file changes. Read-only review permits no source edits, persisted reports, or mutation-based experiments.
- Neither mode requires a closing approval request: deliver the requested brief, analysis, or prioritized findings.

## Inspect, correct, and converge

Inspect the changed surface and exercise the affected flow using permitted tools and runtime.
Compare results with the requested outcome, established identity or chosen thesis, and relevant functionality/accessibility constraints.
Separate visual judgment from mechanical facts; passing automated checks do not prove coherent composition.

Batch related defects into causal corrections, then reinspect changed behavior and nearby regression risks.
Continue when evidence shows a material blocker, broken requirement, or introduced regression; no fixed inspection ceiling excuses one.
Stop speculative polish once the requested outcome holds; further font experiments or decorative flourishes do not justify reopening a settled task.
For failures dependent on unavailable access, identify the exact prerequisite and separate completed work from blocked acceptance.
Do not repeat identical checks that cannot yield new evidence.

## Stopping criteria

- **Implementation:** scoped requirements are present, the primary changed path works in observed relevant contexts, material states and accessibility paths have been inspected, and no known in-scope blocker or introduced regression remains.
- **Planning:** the brief specifies a coherent job, approach, boundaries, states, and constraints, with consequential unknowns identified.
- **Review:** prioritized findings specify location, observed evidence, user impact, and a concrete remedy; distinguish strengths to preserve from defects.
- **Unavailable facts, intent, authority, or runtime proof:** report exact prerequisites and blocked acceptance separately from finished independent work. Source inspection is not runtime acceptance.

## Retain learning without starting new work

Retain observed conventions, reusable patterns, or recurring pitfalls only when useful to future authorized tasks.
Prefer existing shared components, tokens, tests, or project guidance; no new sidecar, decision archive, or special context file is required.
Update durable guidance only when it describes implemented reality and falls within write scope. Read-only and planning-only work do not authorize these writes.
Do not persist speculative preferences as product facts or silently repair unrelated documentation drift.

Reuse accumulated evidence and direction on later assignments; revise them when new requirements or observations justify it.
After handoff, wait for the next assignment. Do not launch a backlog, monitor the project, or continue redesigning independently.
