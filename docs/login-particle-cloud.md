# Login logo particle cloud

Managed workspace logos use the ordinary image everywhere; the particle cloud is an optional login enhancement. If particle extraction is unsuitable, the accepted logo still publishes and the login page keeps the contained ordinary image.

## Upload and rendition contract

The Workspace logo control accepts one visible static PNG, JPEG, or WebP file up to 5 MiB. Oriented dimensions must be 1–4096 pixels per axis and at most 16,777,216 pixels total. Small images and arbitrary aspect ratios are valid.

Pipeline 6 converts the source to sRGB, preserves its complete oriented canvas, transparency, internal margins, and aspect ratio, and emits a deterministic PNG. It never upscales; only sources with a long axis above 1024 pixels are downscaled. Consumers use contained presentation instead of cropping or stretching the logo.

The same activation publishes a complete browser-icon family: 16 px and 32 px favicons, a 150 px tile, a 180 px Apple touch icon, 192 px and 512 px application icons, a 512 px maskable icon, and a two-size ICO. Icons are square and opaque, contain the source with safe padding, and choose a light or dark backdrop for contrast. The document head, favicon redirect, web app manifest, browser configuration, navigation, authentication, registration, unlock, welcome, administration, error, and mail surfaces all resolve from the active logo revision.

Particle and static-effect artifacts are generated only when the source is suitable for the effect. Their absence never blocks the ordinary logo or icon bundle. Historical pipeline 1–5 revisions remain readable, but new uploads and migration backfills publish pipeline 6 revisions.

## Rendering and physics

The scene uses Three.js `WebGPURenderer` with a TSL `SpriteNodeMaterial` over one indexed instanced quad mesh: each source-order particle is one sprite instance, the renderer submits one draw, and the indexed quad contributes `2N` triangles. Native WebGPU is primary. A deliberate `webgl2` request selects strict WebGL2 through `WebGPURenderer`; `auto` accepts native WebGPU or the renderer's successful built-in WebGL2 fallback. Source positions, depth, colors, alpha, and seeds remain immutable views of the validated binary artifact. The dynamic source-order cloud motion is a vec2 attribute: 8 bytes per source particle, or 128 KB scheduled at the 16,000-particle ceiling. During initialization, straight source sRGB bytes are decoded once to Linear-sRGB in a separate `Float32Array` cache; each RGB channel is independent of source alpha, particle seed, and theme or backdrop. No CPU background read, theme observer, RGB refresh, or backdrop-dependent color upload participates in rendering. Source alpha remains separate from RGB and is never folded into it.

The deterministic seed assigns approximately 70% of particles to fine dust, 23.5% to medium motes, and 6.5% to large beads. Source alpha controls coverage instead of giving every opaque source pixel the same diameter. The TSL graph adds independent orbits to a coherent flow, analytic sphere lighting, and a twisting explosion that moves particles instead of fading them out. Fine dust starts at 66% opacity and skips sphere-lighting calculations; larger sprites increase coverage without adding particles or draw calls. Bead diffuse and glint geometry uses bounded scalar illumination of each bead's source RGB, preserving source-colored lighting rather than applying a backdrop-derived tint. Core alpha remains independent: source alpha is multiplied by the existing seed opacity (0.66–0.94), then lifecycle and coverage factors are applied without premultiplying RGB. The TSL material emits direct sRGB with straight (unpremultiplied) RGB and alpha; `outputColorSpace=SRGBColorSpace` and `toneMapping=NoToneMapping` encode no additional transform. The material uses `premultipliedAlpha:false` with `NormalBlending`, while the canvas uses `premultipliedAlpha:true`; the browser therefore performs the intended native source-over composition over the real login backdrop. There is no forced contrast target: exact source/backdrop matches can be low-contrast, including white-on-white or black-on-black cases.

At most 512 large beads have physical spring motion and collision response. A spatial hash searches adjacent cells, visits each pair once, and caps candidate visits per bead. Squared-distance rejection skips square roots for particles outside a brush, blast, or contact radius; included forces and contacts retain the original arithmetic and ordering. Seed phases and damping are precomputed. The simulation uses a 120 Hz fixed step with at most four steps per rendered frame; a suspended tab cannot create a catch-up backlog. Fine dust remains entirely TSL animated. Bead displacements use one preallocated dynamic vec2 attribute update (8 bytes per source particle, at most 128 KB scheduled at 16,000 particles). The color cache occupies 16 bytes per particle (256 KB at the 16,000-particle ceiling), with no per-frame color upload. There are no per-frame particle allocations, mesh instances, lighting passes, bloom passes, or CSS filters over the canvas.

Pointer samples feed one continuous brush, with a 65 ms follow time and a 180 ms release. Mouse, pen, and captured touch motion share a 31.25% strength gain and a 42 CSS-pixel travel cap (previously 32). The brush radius gains 12%, bounded at 72 CSS pixels. Dust evaluates that brush at its current idle-plus-explosion position; the bounded physical beads use their current simulated position. The brush has a compact radius and a seed-dependent dust response. Moving the pointer over an exploded particle's original anchor cannot drag the remote particle, and brush motion never replaces the explosion's scatter/return path. The previous six overlapping GPU impulse fields could move remote particles together and snap when ring slots were recycled; the TSL graph now evaluates one local field per particle.

Each accepted click samples a scale from 0.90–1.45 once. That scale multiplies the existing 100–240 CSS-pixel base blast radius, giving a bounded 90–348 CSS-pixel range across viewport sizes. It also scales bead impulse strength; both populations share the same radius cap and chosen scale. The six-blast capacity, 2.8-second lifetime, particle count, and physics budget are unchanged.

The renderer initializes the source-color cache before its first frame and never samples or recolors particles from the backdrop. It does not attach a theme observer or recalculate the cache when the surrounding UI changes. Theme changes update the surrounding UI and backdrop only; cached source colors and alpha remain unchanged, and no color buffer is recalculated or uploaded. The direct-sRGB material/canvas flags preserve native source-over composition over whichever login backdrop is active, including explicit custom backgrounds. During temporary hidden or offscreen periods, the committed canvas, backend lease, and scene resources remain mounted while the Tres update/render loop and pointer work stop; static coverage remains until a resumed frame commits. Reduced motion, descriptor replacement, backend loss, initialization failure, or unmount are terminal boundaries that remove the canvas and retire/dispose the renderer and scene resources. Initialization failures, strict-native WebGPU rejection, and runtime context/device loss keep authentication usable through static fallback.

The default orbit illustration is omitted when a managed logo is configured. Explicit custom login backgrounds are preserved.

After terminal ordinary authentication succeeds, the login loader may show a decorative TS Epistle open-book illustration for a 900 ms book-opening sequence before redirect. It is auth-success-only: failed login, TFA, password-reset, verification, and other non-terminal states do not show it. Reduced-motion styling collapses the animation while preserving the same success-only gate; the art never participates in authentication.

## Renderer and backend policy

The shared scene path is `WebGPURenderer` plus TSL `SpriteNodeMaterial`, an indexed instanced quad, and source-order attributes. It does not use legacy GLSL files or point primitives. Native WebGPU is the primary backend. A deliberate WebGL2 profile uses `forceWebGL` and requires strict WebGL2; the default `auto` profile accepts native WebGPU or only a successful built-in WebGL2 fallback from `WebGPURenderer`. Backend identity is committed before the first visible particle frame.

One indexed instanced quad draw renders all `N` sprites as `2N` triangles. The source-order vec2 motion attribute schedules 8 bytes per particle, 128 KB at 16,000 particles; source attributes and the decoded color cache remain immutable. The TSL material emits direct sRGB, uses `premultipliedAlpha:false` and `NormalBlending`, and the renderer deliberately selects `LinearSRGBColorSpace` with `NoToneMapping` so Three's common renderer presents those already-encoded values without a second conversion pass. The premultiplied canvas then supplies native source-over composition. Temporary inactivity retains the same canvas and lease generation while application update, render, draw, motion-transfer, and upload work remains stopped. Three's common renderer may continue page-global bookkeeping RAF callbacks; these advisory callbacks are not particle simulation or draw work. Terminal boundaries retire and dispose the lease and resources. Static fallback covers initialization, artifact, capability, and runtime failures.

The `performance-webgpu` and `performance-webgl2` Playwright projects are the profiling entry points. The deployed strict WebGPU run rendered active animation at frame p95/p99 17.2/19.8 ms with callback CPU p95 0.6 ms, but failed the complete profile because 5 of 20 cold starts exceeded the 2-second gate and repeated headless contexts produced Chromium SharedImage backing failures; a follow-up run exhausted the 180-second harness timeout during the same context churn. The corrected forced-WebGL2 profile completed with strict backend identity, 20 of 20 cold starts (p95 696.9 ms), and callback CPU p95 1.0 ms, but failed the active target at frame p95/p99 148.6/186.8 ms with only 87 interval samples. These headless results do not establish representative hardware WebGL2 throughput. The completed WebGL2 profile observed zero application updates, render invocations, draws, scheduled motion bytes, and uploads while hidden/offscreen; nonzero page-global RAF counts remain advisory. Benchmark observation is caller-owned: Playwright installs its hook before app load and may count actual callbacks plus observed public renderer-info draw deltas and public upload API calls/bytes. If a browser/API does not expose upload observation, the value remains unavailable rather than being reported as zero. See the [Three.js WebGPU renderer guide](https://threejs.org/manual/en/webgpurenderer.html).

The historical audit retained a single contiguous motion transfer and bounded physical beads independently of density. Those historical WebGL measurements remain dated evidence only; they do not validate the current WebGPU/WebGL2 backend path or imply that native profiling has passed.

Current bundle-cost note from the verified production build: the lazy scene measured 1490.7 KiB raw and 404.0 KiB gzip. The former 950 KiB raw / 260 KiB gzip caps predated the required TresJS plus Three.js WebGPU/classic-renderer closure; the planner accepted the measured ceilings with approximately 5% headroom. This is a budget-cap correction, not a runtime improvement, and initial-route budgets remain unchanged.

## Tuning

- `particle-cloud.ts`: `CLOUD_DUST_FRACTION` and `CLOUD_BEAD_FRACTION` control the population mix used by the TSL graph. `CLOUD_BEAD_LIMIT` caps physical beads independently of source density. Keep the fractions positive and their sum below one.
- `particle-material.ts`: TSL sprite diameter ranges at a 1024 CSS-pixel logo axis are 4–8 px, 8–12 px, and 13–20 px. Coverage and depth adjust these, with a final 22 px cap. When changing the large-bead range, update the collision radius formula in `ParticleCloud.update` too.
- `particle-cloud.ts`: spring stiffness, damping, and the fixed step control weight and settling; the hash cell must remain at least the maximum collision diameter.
- `particle-colors.ts`: one-time source sRGB-to-linear RGB decoding and independent alpha. The initialization cache is source-faithful and independent of source alpha, seed, theme, and backdrop. Core alpha remains `source alpha × seed opacity`, with lifecycle and coverage applied in the TSL material; RGB is never premultiplied in the color cache. The material explicitly emits direct sRGB once, with `premultipliedAlpha:false` and `NormalBlending`; the renderer's `LinearSRGBColorSpace` setting is an intentional public-API control signal that suppresses a redundant common-renderer output conversion, not a claim that the fragment values are linear. `NoToneMapping` and a `premultipliedAlpha:true` canvas preserve native source-over composition, and low contrast is accepted when source and backdrop match.
- `useLogoPointer.ts`: input sampling, bounded six-slot impulse/explosion buffers, and explosion scale limits.
- `particle-brush.ts`: continuous brush follow/release and maximum deflection. The TSL graph and physics consume this same preallocated state.

Historical color-cache and physics audit on 2026-09-08:

- A warmed Bun microbenchmark used 16,000 source particles, 512 physical beads, 1,200 simulated frames, and nine alternating baseline/optimized repetitions. Median idle work decreased from 153.6 to 120.0 ms; brush-and-blast work decreased from 222.7 to 146.9 ms. All compared positions, velocities, and motion values were identical. - The historical GPU transform-feedback comparison used cached output and a frozen copy of the GLSL color calculation from that audit for 1,920 color/alpha/seed/surface samples in both Chromium and WebKit. - The first optimized 16,000-particle stress run measured callback CPU p95 0.4 ms and frame p95/p99 20.4/22.2 ms.
